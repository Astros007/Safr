About routing & location

Routes and directions: Valhalla / OpenStreetMap. Place search: Photon and US Census. Weather: Open-Meteo. These community services may have gaps or become unavailable.

Your selected endpoints and route shapes are sent to the routing service for directions and road details. Route bounds are sent to TxDOT, Houston and OpenStreetMap services for public traffic, crash, sidewalk, lighting and curb data. Community reports store the hazard point you select and your note, associated with your signed-in account; your account identifier is not shown on the community map. Search text and the search area go to Photon. House-number searches also go through Pathfinder to the US Census address service; its locations are estimates along a street. Weather uses a rounded location. GPS stays on this device except when used as a route start or to reroute. No trip history is stored.

GPS guidance works while this page is open. Keep your screen on; browser navigation may pause in the background. Mapped sidewalk and crossing information may be incomplete.

How we compare routes

Each street segment gets an exposure score for your profile. The default selects the lowest total among assessed options. The junction priority first minimizes major junctions without mapped signals, then all major junctions, then the exposure score. Travel time breaks ties.

  • Fast roadsDistance on roads with a mapped limit of 35 mph or higher. Where limits are missing, road class provides an estimate.
  • Traffic volumeDistance along TxDOT roads carrying at least 10,000 vehicles a day on average. These are annual counts, not live traffic. Major-road class is used when counts are missing.
  • Major intersectionsMapped junctions with major roads or roads with four or more lanes. Nearby nodes are grouped; signal information is considered where available.
  • Paths and crash historyDedicated paths reduce road exposure. Houston’s 2018–2022 high injury corridors add historical context.

The percentage compares our weighted exposure scores: road distance, speed, volume, intersections, infrastructure, and crash corridors. It is an experimental comparison—not the probability of a crash or a guarantee of safety. Missing map data can affect the result.

Score calculation & sources

Per 100 m: basic road exposure 0.25; road class adds 0–5; speed adds 0–5; mapped volume adds 0.8 at 5,000, 2.5 at 10,000, or 4 at 20,000 vehicles/day. Dedicated paths and mapped sidewalks or cycle lanes reduce road terms. Each major intersection adds 6 points, or 9 without a mapped signal. Each 100 m near a high injury corridor adds 2 points. Profiles multiply road exposure (1–1.4×) and junction penalties (1–2.5×). Per 100 m, explicitly missing sidewalks add 2–15 points; unknown sidewalk coverage adds 0.25–2; mapped unlit streets add 0.8–2. Raised curbs add 3–30 points and steps add 2–100 per segment. Wheelchair mode excludes assessed options with mapped steps or wheelchair restrictions. Community hazard penalties are capped at 15 points per hazard type per roughly 40 m cell. Coefficients are design choices, not a validated injury model.

Help & privacy

Find a destination

Enter a place name or house number and street. Add a city or ZIP code for an address farther away. If a place is missing, tap the map and choose “Go here.”

Start your trip

Choose a route, then tap Start walk or Start ride. Allow location access when asked. Keep the page open and your screen on for GPS directions.

Understand the comparison

The recommended route has the lowest estimated road exposure among the assessed options, unless you selected fewer crossings. It is not a guarantee of safety. Always follow signs, crossing signals, and current conditions. Access and opening hours may be missing.

Your information

Pathfinder does not save your trip history. Search and routing providers receive the places or coordinates needed to answer your requests. Settings are saved on this device. Reports you submit are shared with people who can access the app. You can withdraw your reports from their map pins.

If something stops working

Search, new routes, maps, and weather need an internet connection. Retry a failed search. For weak GPS, move to an open area; if location access is blocked, update the browser’s site permissions. Directions cannot reliably continue when the browser is in the background.
